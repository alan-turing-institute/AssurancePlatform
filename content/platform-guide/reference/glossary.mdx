## Argument and evidence

- **Argument:** The stated reasoning that connects a goal, its supporting claims and the evidence offered for them.
- **Assurance:** Work that builds justified confidence in a system through questions, actions, argument and evidence.
- **Assurance case:** A structured, reviewable argument about a system in a stated context.
- **Claim:** A proposition about a system or project that can be supported or challenged.
- **Goal:** The broad proposition at the top of a case. The editor creates `G1` when a new case is made.
- **Strategy:** An explanation of how a goal or claim is broken into parts that can be supported.
- **Property claim:** A narrower proposition about the system or project that a reviewer can test or challenge.
- **Evidence:** An artefact or result offered for a particular claim. Its relevance, reliability and sufficiency still need assessment.
- **Evidence quality:** A judgement about whether evidence is relevant to a claim, comes from a credible source and is sufficient for the proposed conclusion.
- **Relevance:** The degree to which evidence addresses the particular claim to which it is linked.
- **Reliability:** The trustworthiness of an evidence source, method and record.
- **Sufficiency:** Whether the available evidence is enough for the claim and its stated scope.
- **Context:** A definition or boundary needed to understand where a claim applies.
- **Assumption:** A condition taken as given in an argument and open to challenge if it fails.
- **Justification:** A reason for including a claim, choice or decomposition in the argument.
- **Defeater:** An element used to challenge another element. Its name uses `C` before its normal type prefix, such as `CP1`.
- **Away goal:** An element that cites a goal or other valid target in a referenced case.
- **Module:** A reference to another case used as part of an argument.
- **Assertion status:** The author's declared position on an assertion: Asserted, Needs support, Assumed, Axiomatic or Defeated. As cited is reserved and is not computed automatically in this build.

## Project reasoning

- **Critical reflection:** Examination of a project's assumptions, possible harms, argument and evidence, repeated as the project develops.
- **Deliberation:** Careful discussion of options, values and trade-offs with people who bring relevant perspectives.
- **Stakeholder engagement:** Involving people who use, build, govern or may be affected by a system while its questions and choices can still change.
- **Ethical principle:** A broad aim, such as fairness, that starts deliberation but needs project-specific interpretation.
- **Fairness:** A concern with how a system affects people's rights, interests and outcomes, specified for a particular use setting.
- **Explainability:** The ability to give a useful account of system behaviour to the people who need it, considering the wider use setting as well as the model.
- **Accountability:** Clear responsibility for project decisions and a record that lets others understand and challenge them.
- **Proportionality:** Giving inquiry and evidence work attention in relation to a project's likely effects and use setting.
- **Specification:** Turning a broad principle into a statement precise enough to guide decisions for a particular system.
- **Operationalisation:** Identifying, weighing, specifying, revising and implementing principles in project activity.
- **Justified trust:** Confidence grounded in inspectable reasons and evidence, with limits stated.

## Platform collaboration

- **Stakeholder:** A person or group who uses, builds, governs or may be affected by the system.
- **Trustworthy and Ethical Assurance (TEA):** The practice of building a reviewable argument, with evidence and stakeholder reasoning, for a system in context.
- **Working case:** The editable case available to people and teams with case permission.
- **Team role:** A person's membership role within a team; it does not automatically confer case access.
- **Case permission:** A direct or team grant to view, comment, edit or administer a working case.
- **Comment:** Discussion attached to a case or element, kept outside the public published snapshot.
- **Live update:** A case event that prompts another open editor view to refresh after a change.
- **Public snapshot:** A published version available on Discover under a slug, separate from the working case.
- **Discover:** The public area where published case versions can be found and read.
- **Slug:** The readable path segment used for a published case page.
- **Backup:** A separate exported copy of case data, such as JSON saved to Google Drive.
- **Machine integration:** A registered external service that uses scoped API tokens and case grants.
- **Health evidence:** An append-only, hash-chained record of one runtime check result against a property claim, with a metric, value, threshold, verdict and provenance.
- **API token:** A secret beginning `teap_` that a machine client sends as a bearer credential and that TEA shows once on issue.
- **Scope:** A named kind of machine action allowed to an integration; case permission still controls which case it can use.
- **Trash:** The recovery area for deleted cases before the 30-day purge cutoff.

For the controls behind these terms, see [platform basics](/docs/platform-guide/reference/01-platform-basics) and [element types](/docs/platform-guide/reference/02-element-types).

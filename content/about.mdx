The Trustworthy and Ethical Assurance (TEA) Platform is an open-source project developed by researchers at the Alan Turing Institute and the Centre for Assuring Autonomy at the University of York. It helps project teams develop and communicate structured arguments about whether a system meets a goal, and what evidence supports that judgement.

## What an assurance case is for

A team might need to explain how a system meets a goal such as fairness, safety or explainability. That requires more than a final assertion. The team must define the goal in the project's context, show how it was addressed in design and testing, and make the supporting evidence inspectable. An assurance case gives those claims and evidence a structure that colleagues and stakeholders can challenge and revise.

![An example assurance case with a goal, supporting claims and evidence](/images/about-page-example.svg)

The diagram is a simple example. A top-level goal states what is to be assured. Strategies and property claims break that goal into more specific claims. Evidence supports those claims. Context and assumptions explain the scope and reasoning. A real case can change as a project learns more.

## The platform and curriculum

The [case builder](https://assuranceplatform.azurewebsites.net/) lets a team create and edit a case in a visual interface. Members can be granted view, comment, edit or administrative access. They can discuss individual elements and publish a frozen snapshot when they want to share a case publicly. The [Discover page](https://assuranceplatform.azurewebsites.net/discover) currently lists published assurance cases.

The [TEA curriculum](/docs/curriculum) introduces assurance case ideas and guides people through the tool. Its trainee stage starts with reading an example, then moves towards building a case and working with others. The [technical guide](/docs/technical-guide) is for developers and operators.

## Community and extensions

The platform supports collaboration through teams, sharing and comments. Published cases let a wider audience inspect a snapshot without gaining access to the live working case. Community discussion can continue through the project's [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/alan-turing-institute/AssurancePlatform/discussions).

Plugins already have a foundation in the code, and the health plugin uses it. The [plugin foundation](/docs/technical-guide/architecture/plugin-foundation) describes the current extension mechanism.

## Support and partners

The ongoing development and maintenance of TEA is supported by the Alan Turing Institute. The existing project record also names support from the EPSRC Networks of Cardiovascular Digital Twins programme, the BRAID programme, the Assuring Autonomy International Programme and UKRI's Trustworthy Autonomous Systems Hub. The Centre for Assuring Autonomy and the Digital Twins Hub are shown as collaborators in the current site material.
